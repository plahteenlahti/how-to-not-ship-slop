01 · The feeling

You can smell it
before you can
explain it.

The page that could belong to any product. The post that tells you what it's about, then tells you again. Nothing is wrong with it. That's the problem.

Merriam-Webster · Word of the Year
SLOP
2025. "Low-quality digital content produced in quantity by artificial intelligence." The word stuck because everyone already knew the feeling.
The uncomfortable part
The AI isn't
producing slop.
YOU ARE.

Slop is a workflow failure wearing a technology costume. The model gives you exactly what you asked for, when you didn't really ask for anything.

What it actually is

Content optimized to exist.
Not to be read or used.

The giveaway is always an absence: no specific person, no stake, no point of view, no detail that could only have come from someone who was actually there.

Why the machine defaults to it
average average average average AVERAGE
Most probable = most average. A thin prompt asks for the center of the distribution. The center is, by definition, the part nobody remembers.
So the whole job is one thing

Drag your work away from the center.

Everything that follows is a way of doing exactly that.

01
The spine
Three things a model can't give you
ContextTasteAn owner
Slop is what you get when all three are missing
A · RAW MATERIAL

Context

The specific facts, real data, the thing that happened on Tuesday. Without it, the model reaches for the generic version of your topic.

B · JUDGMENT

Taste

The model has read everything and prefers nothing. It writes the cliché and the fresh line with equal enthusiasm. Taste says: not that, the other one.

C · A HUMAN

An owner

Someone who will defend every word and pixel. Not approve. Defend. Slop is what no one decided on purpose.

02
Practice · words
Not shipping written slop
Habit 01

Generate from
material, not a topic.

"Write about onboarding" → the median.

Your support tickets + the number that made you angry → something only you could have written. Output is capped by input. A topic is nearly nothing.
Habit 02

Decide the point before you prompt.

A model can't hold an opinion you haven't given it, and it will never tell you that you don't have one. Using the model to find your take is how you publish the absence of one.

Habit 03 · the most reliable tell

Specificity is the antidote.

✕"Businesses today leverage cutting-edge solutions."
✓"We cut onboarding from 9 steps to 3. Activation rose 14%."

Test: could this sentence sit, word for word, on a competitor's page? Then cut it or make it specific.

Habit 04

Edit destructively. Hardest at the open and the close.

KILL THE TELLS

rule-of-three lists "not just X, it's Y" hedging throat-clearing intros summary that restates the intro em-dash pile-ups
Habit 05 · the cheapest check

Read it out loud.

Anything you wouldn't actually say to a colleague over coffee is slop. Your mouth catches the AI register before your eyes do.

03
Practice · pixels
Not shipping design slop
The visual center of the distribution

The design mean.

Inter. Purple gradient. Centered hero. Three icon cards. Testimonial carousel. None of it bad. All of it average.

Habit 06

Constrain
before you
generate.

Decide type, colour, layout and motion as deliberate choices. Then subtract the defaults with negative constraints:
No InterNo centred heroNo purple gradientNo card grid
Habit 07

References, not adjectives.

"Modern" and "clean" constrain nothing. Every product claims them. A real reference ("lay it out like an editorial spread") carries a thousand borrowed decisions. An adjective carries none.

Habit 08 · the distinctiveness test

The lineup test.

If a stranger can't tell which one is yours, you shipped slop, however polished each pixel is.

04
The deeper shift
What the tools actually changed
The scarce skill moved
Production was the bottleneck.
Now it's judgment.

Making is nearly free. So the valuable part is knowing what's worth making, and telling the good version from the average one. Taste was always valuable. Now it's the whole job.

The trap that makes most of the slop

Don't spend the speed on volume.
Spend it on quality.

Slop is, economically, a quantity play. Make fewer things, or make the same thing five times until it's good. Compete on the one axis the machine can't: whether anyone gives a damn.

In practice · three unglamorous disciplines
01

One owner

Every artifact has a single accountable human who can defend every choice in it. "No one decided this" is the definition of slop.

02

Codify taste

Write it down where the tool can use it: a voice file, a design system, a folder of good-versus-bad examples. Make "good" a thing you can point at.

03

Last-mile pass

A human edit on anything that ships. Most attention on what faces other people: claims, the open, the close, the gut-check.

An honest counterpoint

Don't become the em-dash police.

Detectors are barely better than coin flips. A tic isn't a crime. The problem was never the em-dash. It was the absence of a person. Plenty of slop has no em-dashes at all.

And the other half of honesty

Good-enough is sometimes good enough.

The status update, the throwaway script, the draft no one else reads. Ship the average version and save your taste for where it counts. Caring about everything equally is its own failure.

When production is free…

…competent-and-average is worth roughly nothing.

It's the new floor. What keeps its value is what the average doesn't have: a point of view, specific knowledge, real taste, and a person willing to stand behind it.
