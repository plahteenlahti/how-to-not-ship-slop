11:00 Successful subscription businesses optimize relentlessly based on customer feedback. Building great products is largely about shortening the time between learning something from customers and acting on it. The faster a team understands what customers want, which features they value, and what they’re willing to pay for, the faster it can improve the product. Over time this compounds into a better user experience and stronger monetization.

React Native is a strong fit for this. EAS and Codemagic let teams ship many changes instantly without app reviews, and RevenueCat lets you experiment with pricing, offerings, and paywalls without a new app release.

But React Native is not a prerequisite for strong monetization. The spread inside each stack is still bigger than the gap between them.

Key learning 3 / 5
Key learning 3 / 5

Monetization is a product problem

If monetization gains persist for two months, the explanation probably isn’t a better payment process. Realistically, it’s about the product being great.

Most customers are not engineers. For them, it’s more important if the product solves the problem, not how.

14:00 If monetization gains persist for two months, the explanation probably isn’t a better payment process. It’s about the product being great. Monetizing well beyond the first month means these are valuable products users want to pay for, and to get there you need to be a product-oriented person or team.

Something in React Native makes product-oriented teams choose it. When I posted about this, all the people who challenged the results were engineers. From an engineering perspective, React Native is easy to challenge: there’s a bunch of weird stuff compared to something like Swift.

But most customers are not engineers. And even if they are, they don’t approach the products they use from an engineering perspective. For them, it matters if the product solves the problem, not how.

Key learning 4 / 5
Key learning 4 / 5

Play long-term games

Your app business should not be about stacks and hacks, at least if you’re in it for the long term. The longer you keep your customers, the more money they make.

Customer loyalty is earned through product value, not technology choices.

17:30 Stack choice has very little impact on whether customers stay subscribed. Your app business should not be about stacks and hacks, at least if you’re in it for the long term. You can build a decent business by monetizing a loved product with a small but significant audience, as long as you keep those customers.

Frameworks help teams move faster and experiment more often. But they can’t replace the fundamentals: understanding customer problems, building features people need, and delivering enough value to justify an ongoing subscription. The longer you keep your customers, the more money they make. Customer loyalty is earned through product value, not technology choices.

Key learning 5 / 5
Key learning 5 / 5

Valuable customers beat more customers

The apps that perform best are not necessarily the ones attracting the most customers. They’re often the ones creating the most value for the customers they already have.

Each customer becomes more valuable. Not because you’re extracting more from them, but because you’re giving them more reasons to stay.

20:30 The best-performing apps are not necessarily the ones attracting the most customers. They’re often the ones creating the most value for the customers they already have.

That’s good news for builders. Success isn’t about growth at all costs. You don’t need to win every download or dominate every category. Sometimes the biggest opportunities come from deeply understanding a smaller group of customers and building something they’re genuinely happy to pay for. Each customer becomes more valuable, not because you’re extracting more from them, but because you’re giving them more reasons to stay.
