- The fundamentals of app market place 
  - So you built an app, it’s approved by apple and google and now you want to monetize it, how should you go about it
    - Slap a price on it = works, but you only get paid once, and you miss on a lot of users who would find your app useful but can’t justify buying it without knowing how it works
    - Make it available for free, you can use it for free (to some level) but allow people to pay to unlock features
      - This is called the soft paywall
    - Make it available for free but don’t let them use any of it until they pay something for it
      - This is called a hard paywall
      - To offer free access, you need to have for example a trial
    - We have data on this what works the best in our yearly report. Turns out, these two perform very similarly in long term, but hard paywall makes more money upfront, but eventually people churn turning the numbers pretty much equal
  - Now you figured how you want you monetize your app, it’s a freemium, but what different methods for monetizing do you have?
    - First non-consumable in-app purchases. Very similar to slapping a price on it, you buy it and own it for life. 
    - Second we have subscriptions, so for example weekly, monthly, and yearly subscriptions. Allows user to use the app during that period, if they continue paying they can continue using. Everyone knows these. 
      - We are leaning towards this, since getting paid monthly makes it easy for us to see us working on the app every month, putting a direct number on the value of our work
    - Third we have consumable in-app purchases, a mechanic that allows you to buy the same thing as many times as you want. Good for game mechanics, and stuff like in-app currencies. We could probably throw this on top of our app as an extra monetization method
    - Ads! Make your customer the product. We might throw this in our example app
  - So you chose to go with subscriptions and you want to be data oriented and choose the best subscription lengths that people will want to convert with
    - You need yearly, because you would like if your customers paid a lot of money and then stayed on for long. There’s also a benefit that Apple and google both reward you for longer subscription, if customers stay subscribed, you get to keep more (drops from 30 to 15%)
    - But then, should you have weekly or monthly subscription? You’ve seen a lot of weekly subs, so maybe that is the way?
      - Well data says that yes for first week, but churn is higher on these, causing you lose money
      - So you choose monthly instead
  - Now's the time choose if you want to have trial in your subscription
    - For a hard paywall this would be necessary
    - But soft paywall, it’s a good way to give people a chance to try out your apps premium features
    - But what should your trial length be?
      - 3 days?
      - Two weeks?
    - Well we can once again answer this with data. Turns out that trend is to offer shorter subscription periods (probably because people forget to cancel them, making you more money). BUT, longer trials actually make more money long term (probably because customers feel like they managed to use the app to its full extent)
- Now you have your monetization figured out, the next step is to wire this all into your app. How to go about that?
  - Your app is on two platforms, ios and android
  - Both platforms have their own SDKs, iOS has StoreKit and Android has Google play billing
  - Using these separately in your Flutter apps would require quite a bit of tinkering, so maybe you want a cross-platform solution
  - For that you have the official plugin in_app_purchase 
    - I have not personally used it so I can’t really comment on how well it works
    - But I can comment on things you need to build yourself if you end up using them
    - The most difficult part being your own backend, that handles verifying the purchases so that people don’t just go around doing enabling your precious features from the client side
    - You also probably want some analytics, like what is your conversion rate, churn, cost of acquisition if you’re getting users with ads, lifetime value etc
    - Now you can codex yourself a platform to do those, but you really that maintaining that is worth you time?
    - Because if you’re building an app that you love, then maybe you should focus on that instead
  - And here’s where the previously mentioned RevenueCat affiliation comes in, we do all of that backend stuff for you, plus quite a lot of other things
    - We do the analytics, but also A/B testing, targeting, experiments, so you can easily see which prices, products, subscription lengths at other things enable you to monetize
    - We also support making your content purchasable from other places. Let’s say for example that you want to allow customer to subscribe from web, avoiding the Apple commission and using for example Stripe, we support that and make it easy to integrate
    - Then we also have great paywall tools, meaning a visual builder, but also an agentic one, that you can use to prompt your paywall into existence. Best is that you don’t have to make new app releases to get to your updated paywall in front of customers, our SDK makes it easy to update the dashboard, and have it live when customer next time opens their app
  - For all of that you pay nothing, until you actually start making money. After you make 2.5k a month, we bill you 1%. That’s a pretty cheap prize for making a decent amount of money.

- What does a good paywall look like?
  - There’s multiple ways to build a good paywall but here’s some good suggestions, these are mostly also in our paywall builder as skills so you could get something like this already from the start
    - First show benefits, not features. Push notifications is a feature, getting reminders to complete your lesson is a benefit
    - Show social proof, if customers love your app, slots few of those reviews in the paywall
    - Third is keep it simple and highlight your trial. Have only 1-2 options in your paywall, monthly and yearly, and clearly tell which one of them has a trial and how long it is

- Now we’ve monetized our app in multiple ways already, our conversions are pretty good, but sometimes users churn, what can we do about that?
  - Churn management is a whole topic of it’s own but I’ll leave you with two really good things

    - Once you done that, you can actually enable it in RevenueCat
    - And after that RevenueCat can show retention messages like these when user goes to unsubscribe

This talk dives into the practical realities of monetizing Flutter apps through subscriptions and in-app purchases, covering both the technical implementation and the design decisions that determine whether users actually convert. We'll walk through how IAP works under the hood on iOS and Android, the quirks of integrating it cleanly in a cross-platform Flutter codebase, and the third-party tools that can save you weeks of headache around receipt validation, entitlements, and analytics. Beyond the code, we'll look at what makes a paywall feel inviting rather than pushy, how pricing and trial structures influence user behavior, and the small UX details that separate apps people happily pay for from ones they immediately uninstall. By the end, you'll have a clear mental model of the entire monetization stack in Flutter and a practical sense of where to invest your time to actually start earning revenue from your app.
